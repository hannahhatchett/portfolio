Bottle Cap

Brand identity and website redesign for a Nashville pub.

Agency Element 47
Nashville, TN
Client Bottle Cap
Nashville, TN
My Role UX Design Intern
Timeline 2 months · 2024

Situation

Bottle Cap is a popular pub in the heart of downtown Nashville. Its old website was years out of date. For context, the menu was a fuzzy screenshot of a PDF, the events page was a wall of type with no hierarchy, and the location page only gave an address with no hyperlink to Maps.

Task

I joined Element 47, a branding agency in Nashville, TN, as a UX design intern to redesign the information architecture and build a brand identity that fit the pub.

I ran the design process on my own, from discovery through wireframes and visual design to handoff. I handed off low fidelity screens to our contractor at the time, who was hired to bring them to high fidelity. So I had to lead that collaboration and give clear directions.

Action

The project ran in three phases. Discovery and research came first, then brand direction and client advocacy, then wireframes and high fidelity design. Each phase built on the one before it.

Phase 01

Discovery & Research

At the start I had a client and not much else. I did not know what the Bottle Cap brand should be, what was failing on the existing site, or what people wanted from it. I needed to understand the business and the market it competed in before I designed anything.

I needed a clear picture of the client's priorities and frustrations, and of how Bottle Cap sat next to other Nashville bars.

I worked with the Bottle Cap crew to surface what they wanted, what they valued, and what frustrated them about the current site. Then I ran a competitive analysis of other Nashville bars and pubs, including M.L. Rose, Burger Bar, Black Sheep Lodge, The Pharmacy, Black Tap, and Bad Luck Burger Club. I looked at their visual systems, their site structures, and the conventions they all shared.

Most of those bars looked alike. That gave Bottle Cap room to stand out. The research also surfaced a problem I would have to handle in the next phase. The client liked the look of one competitor and wanted to copy it.

Phase 02

Brand Direction & Client Advocacy

The client wanted to imitate a competing Nashville bar. I understood the appeal. They responded to that bar's energy and tone. But imitating another business's visual identity is unfavorable for both businesses involved, and it would have handed Bottle Cap a borrowed identity instead of a brand. I had to work out who Bottle Cap was and build an identity from that, not reproduce someone else's answer.

Moving forward with this logic, I needed to bring Bottle Cap to an original direction without dismissing their instincts, and build a visual foundation that belonged to them.

Instead of telling the client that copying a competitor was a bad idea, I used their own goals to reframe the conversation. They wanted to stand out in Nashville, build a regular crowd, and feel local. So I presented other popular competitors, talked through the most desirable aspects, and came up with an original direction. By grounding the pitch in their own goals, it made the redirection a collaborative effort instead of a correction. That direction became the foundation for the whole redesign.

Phase 03

Wireframing & High Fidelity Design

With the brand direction approved, I had to turn it into a working site. The old site had real usability problems. Navigation changed from page to page, content had no hierarchy, and key information like the menu, specials, events, and location was hard to find or missing entirely.

I created wireframes that fixed the structure in Figma, and then I had to get the contractor to bring those screens to high fidelity without losing the brand system.

With the redesign, navigation now stays consistent across pages. The menu, specials, events, and location each got their own page for ease of use. Once Element 47 approved the wireframes, I handed off to the contracted UI designer. I gave detailed guidance on the brand system, reviewed the high fidelity screens for consistency, and helped in the iteration process until they matched the standard set in the brand phase.

From this, I delivered a complete website redesign, ready for shipment. It held together as a brand, it fixed the structural problems, and it matched what the client desired.

Result

The redesigned site launched at the end of my internship. My work wrapped at launch, so I have no numbers from after launch and no further client feedback to share here.

What I can say is that the delivered site fixed every structural problem I found in phase three. Navigation is consistent. The menu is responsive instead of an embedded PDF. The location page has a map. The brand system carries through every page. Bottle Cap went from a dated site to an original identity with a website that works for guests looking for hours, a menu, and events. I am happy to share that their live site is the work I contributed in 2024.

Reflection

If I were to start over, I would expand the research to include bar patrons. The stakeholders gave me a strong read on the business, but testing with real users earlier would have validated the wireframe decisions before I committed to any designs.

Redirecting the client on brand direction taught me the most. Good design advocacy is not about overruling a client. It is about helping them get to a better version of what they already want.
